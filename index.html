Two soft ways to make ions

Electrospray (ESI) and matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization (MALDI) turn large, fragile molecules such as peptides and proteins into gas-phase ions without breaking them. Switch between them with the menu at the top.

Electrospray

Taylor cone. The metal capillary is held at +3 to 5 kV and the inlet at ground. Positive ions in the liquid move to the surface at the tip and pull it into a cone; a jet from its tip breaks into charged droplets. Spraying starts above the onset voltage Von = √(rcγcos49°/2ε0)·ln(4d/rc), which grows with the surface tension γ. That is why water alone sprays poorly and methanol or acetonitrile is added; far above onset the spray breaks into several jets and corona discharge becomes likely.

Evaporation and fission. Warm drying gas evaporates the solvent but the charge stays, so the droplet shrinks until its charge reaches the Rayleigh limit, qR = 8π√(ε0γr³), where charge repulsion matches surface tension. It then splits, shedding about 2% of its mass but 15% of its charge, and the cycle repeats until the droplets are nanometers across.

Ion release. Small ions are pushed out of a tiny, highly charged droplet by the field at its surface (ion evaporation, Iribarne and Thomson). Peptides and proteins stay until the last solvent is gone and keep the remaining protons on their basic sites (charged residue, Dole).

Why many charge states. Droplets differ in size and charge, so identical molecules end up with different numbers of protons, each giving its own peak at m/z = (M + n·mH)/n. Two neighboring peaks are enough to find n and M: n = (m/zn+1 − mH)/(m/zn − m/zn+1), then M = n(m/zn − mH). Denatured proteins in acidic solution carry more charges than folded ones, whose average charge is close to 0.078√M.

MALDI

Sample spot. The analyte is mixed with a large excess (about 1,000 to 10,000 times) of a UV-absorbing matrix such as DHB, CHCA or sinapinic acid. As the spot dries, the matrix crystallizes and keeps analyte molecules apart.

Laser and ablation. A nanosecond UV pulse (337 or 355 nm) is absorbed by the matrix, not the analyte. Above a threshold fluence the top of the crystal erupts into a plume; well above it, analyte ions fragment and peaks broaden.

Proton transfer. In the hot plume, M* + M → MH⁺ + (M−H)⁻, then MH⁺ + A → AH⁺ + M, because the analyte holds a proton more strongly than the matrix.

Time of flight. The target at +20 kV pushes positive ions through a grounded grid. Every ion gets the same kinetic energy, zeV = ½mv², so it reaches the detector after t = L√(m/2zeV): lighter ions first. A reflectron (ion mirror) sharpens peptide peaks; large proteins are measured in linear mode. MALDI gives mostly singly charged ions, so the main peak is [M+H]⁺, with small [M+2H]²⁺ and [2M+H]⁺ peaks.
